Every decision that changes the spec gets an entry here, in the same commit as the doc changes it causes. Newest last. IDs use `DEC-` so they never collide with view IDs.

## DEC-01 — Mode A first; Modes B and C after the pilot (Nov 1, 2026)

- **Decision:** Build the full cycle for Mode A before the pilot. Keep Mode B and C seams (contract operations, `claims.mode`, state machines, `client_payer_settings`), but build Stedi claim submission, 999/277CA/835 webhooks, Mode B posting, ERA enrollment and 837 secondaries in M7, after the pilot.
- **Why:** The financial model has 0% of claims on Modes B and C through the pilot. Mode A is the default for onboarding and needs no payer re-enrollment.
- **Exception:** If the Tebra test shows Mode C is the better pilot mode, the Mode C submission path moves into M6 (estimate 2–3 weeks).
- **Affects:** `00-overview.md`, `04-modes.md`, `12-testing.md`, `16-milestones.md`; `ui/README.md` already marks R-03 and CL-10 as after the pilot.

## DEC-02 — The Tebra test decides Mode A or Mode C for the pilot (Nov 1, 2026)

- **Decision:** Run a read-only Tebra test as soon as test access exists (track in `16-milestones.md`). Its results decide the pilot mode.
- **Expected outcome from the published API (April 2026 guide and public WSDL):** Mode A. Tebra can approve encounters through the API (`UpdateEncounterStatus` Review → Approved), which gives Cleanpost a hold and release, but has no way to mark a charge billed, so Mode C would require turning off Tebra's electronic claim submission. Details in `05-adapters.md` and `15-integrations-reference.md`.
- **Open:** Test access. Tebra publishes no sandbox; access needs a Tebra account's system administrator to request a customer key from Tebra Customer Care. Ask the design partner for a test practice with synthetic patients only, and ask Tebra directly for a developer or demo account.

## DEC-10 — Preview may skip two-factor setup (Nov 1, 2026)

- **Decision:** In the preview, the two-factor setup screen (A-03) offers "Skip for now", which lets that one session in; the next sign-in shows the step again. People can still set up real two-factor. Turned on by `PREVIEW_ALLOW_SKIP_2FA=true`.
- **Why:** Demos to people without an authenticator app at hand. The preview holds synthetic data only.
- **Guardrails:** The flag is a startup error unless `CLEANPOST_ENV` is `local` or `preview`, and the skip function refuses to run without it. Remove the flag before any environment that could hold PHI exists; staging and production cannot enable it.
- **Affects:** `packages/config`, `packages/auth/src/session.ts`, A-03 (`apps/web`), `sessions.second_factor_skipped_at`.

## DEC-15 — Void before release only, in M2 (Nov 2, 2026)

## DEC-16 — Write-off approval, amounts and the corrected-claim deadline (Nov 2, 2026)

- **Decision:** D-03 "Request approval" is a `write_off_requests` row plus a `write_off_approval` work item for admins (priority 2, 24 h). The admin approves in the queue, which does the write-off as the admin with the biller recorded (`requestedBy` on the claim event and audit), or declines with a note; the biller gets a notification either way. The tenant's limit is `tenants.settings.writeOffApprovalLimitCents` (default $500) until S-04 exposes it.
- **Decision:** the write-off amount defaults to the denied amount, capped at the claim's balance. The claim balance from the 835 is patient responsibility plus anything denied, so writing off the whole balance would also write off what the patient owes on paid lines.
- **Decision:** D-02's "Corrected claim by" is the client's filing limit for the payer (`client_payer_settings.timely_filing_days`, default 90) counted from the denying payment's date. Payers state their own corrected-claim and appeal windows; this is a planning date, labelled with how it was worked out, until payer rules carry those windows.
- **Also:** D-03 bulk is admin-only and one client at a time (each client's PM has its own write-off codes); each denial's denied amount, one reason and code for all, each claim in its own transaction. The PM write-off codes offered are the client's adjustment mappings with action `write_off`, plus "Another code". A write-off chosen while a suggested correction existed is recorded as `suggestion: not_taken` in the audit; building the correction with the fix is recorded as `fixed` on `claim.correction_built`.
- **Docs:** `docs/09-ui-screens.md` does not say how an approval over the limit is stored or decided; this fills that gap and does not change any screen.

## DEC-17 — Claim status checks before production (Nov 2, 2026)

- **Decision:** one Stedi 276/277 client next to the eligibility client (`packages/eligibility`), switched the same way: Stedi with `STEDI_API_KEY`, else payer-sim in local and preview. Stedi's test keys do not cover claim status, so payer-sim answers the same path from what it received and decided.
- **Decision:** follow-ups (`no_response`) open from the daily sweep when a claim has no 277CA 7 days after it was sent or no payment 30 days after acceptance. The sweep checks claims with an open follow-up once a week; a person may check a claim once a day (C-15 "limited to one a day per claim"). A check that gets no answer within 15 seconds is queued again an hour later; the sweep's checks are not retried (the next sweep asks again), so a slow payer does not fill the dead-letter queue.
- **Decision:** the answer is stored as a `status_277` claim event (codes, amounts, dates; no names or member IDs). It may move a submitted claim to accepted (the payer has it) but never to paid or denied; the 835 decides money. The advice, the suggestion and the task (send records, resubmit) are rules in `packages/domain/src/claim-status`, not an LLM.
- **Decision:** the 276 sends the patient as the subscriber, as the eligibility check does. Dependents (a child on a parent's plan) need the subscriber's own name and birth date from the coverage; that comes with P-02 (edit coverage) in M4.
- **Also:** "usually pays in N days" (W-15) is the median days from acceptance to the payer's answer for the payer's claims answered in the last 180 days, shown from five claims up.

## DEC-18 — Demo time: one clock in local and preview (Nov 3, 2026)

- **Decision:** in local and preview, Cleanpost, the mock PM and the payer simulator share one date, so a demo can move weeks forward in minutes and the whole world follows: claims age, follow-ups come due, payments land on their dates, the work queue's due and overdue counts move. The payer simulator keeps the date (`/clock`); the admin's "+1, +7, +14 days" control (Work and Remittances, "Demo clock") moves it, sets Cleanpost's offset to match, runs the mock PM's clearinghouse, queues a sync per mock-PM practice and runs the daily follow-up sweep once (scheduled jobs run on the real clock, so days skipped at once would otherwise miss it).
- **Decision:** Cleanpost's offset lives in `app.clock` (one row). Business timestamps default to `app.now()` (now plus the offset); code reads business time from `@cleanpost/clock` `now()`, which every process refreshes from `app.clock` every 2 seconds (web per request, worker on a timer) and the browser gets from the page. Sign-in, sessions, rate limits, timeouts, job delays and log times stay on the real clock. The mock PM reads the simulator's clock every 5 seconds and before each clearinghouse run.
- **Decision:** two locks keep it out of staging and production. `app.clock.enabled` is false by default and only the system role can set it; the synthetic seed sets it in local and preview only (staging seeds keep it off). The app role may change only the offset, and the code refuses a non-zero offset, and reads a stored one as zero, when `CLEANPOST_ENV` is staging or production.
- **Also:** `pnpm test:cycle` now runs on demo time: Cleanpost and the PM move with the payer's 45 days and the daily sweep runs at each step. That surfaced two gaps, now closed: a release the PM refuses for good (a Tebra encounter still in Draft or sent back, which Cleanpost builds after the 2-hour quiet period) opens a manual task "Release the charge in {PM}" instead of only a log line; and a claim the PM never bills gets its no-acknowledgment follow-up after 7 days.

## DEC-19 — Demo history: 90 days replayed through the pipeline (Nov 3, 2026)

- **Decision:** the preview opens on a world that has been running for three months, built by the real pipeline, not inserted rows. `pnpm demo:reset` (local and preview) resets the payer simulator and the mock PM through their admin APIs, clears Cleanpost's activity and sets the demo clock (DEC-18) 90 days back, at 9 am Pacific; the worker then replays the 90 days (30 to 45 minutes): each weekday the practices enter 4 to 9 visits each in the mock PM; every three days (daily for the last two weeks) Cleanpost syncs, builds, checks eligibility and scrubs, simulated billers work the queue, the PM's clearinghouse sends and the payer answers, Tebra 835s are uploaded, and the daily follow-up sweep runs. The last step lands on today. The Demo clock bar shows how far it has got and refuses to move the clock meanwhile; a worker that restarts mid-replay carries on from the clock's date.
- **Decision:** the reset clears with the system role, as seeding does (CLAUDE.md rule 1), so it is a command, not a button: it runs in the preview's release step when no history was ever built (`--if-new`), and on request. Kept: tenants, clients, users with their passwords and two-factor, memberships, connections (their sync starts over), rules, mappings, payer settings, saved views. The audit log is append-only, so the earlier world's events stay in it with their dates. The replay itself runs as the app role.
- **Decision:** the simulated billers are the seeded users, so their work shows under their names (approvals, voids, write-offs, mappings, uploads) in the audit log, reports and timelines, as a demo needs; the data is synthetic and this exists only where demo time does. They work the queue as a team that keeps up does, deterministically by item and day: approve most claim reviews a day or more old; apply the scrub's suggested fix to most blocked claims (a bad diagnosis pointer by hand in the editor) and make the same change in the PM by hand, lines the fix dropped included (a correction the PM already billed is no longer needed) (none of the demo PMs takes corrections through its API; pm-mock's admin route stands for the PM's screens), after which the next sync closes the task and the claim goes; void most duplicates; most other work by its due time (docs/08), as a team that mostly meets its SLAs: build the corrected claim (Flow 5) for a denial whose fix is mechanical and resubmit it from the PM; write off a denial nothing can fix (an admin above the billers' limit); let coverage the payer rejects go to the patient (a front-end "Subscriber not found" rejection is resolved as voided in the PM); correct a rejected diagnosis pointer in the PM and send the claim again from there (W-12); settle posting differences; map each new adjustment code once (an admin, from the PM code posted for the same amount); upload Tebra 835s as they arrive. What stays open is mostly the last few days' work, with a few older items, as a real queue has; the newest CO-4 denial is left for the demo's Flow 5 (docs/18).
- **Amended (Nov 6, 2026):** the practices' daily visits draw their defects per visit (5% missing modifier, 2% NCCI conflict, 1% bad pointer), where before a day's handful rounded to none, so the scrub has real catches in the history and the no-hold PM (which bills at 6 pm whatever Cleanpost says) sends some of them uncaught, which come back as CO-4 denials for Flow 5. The billers' fuller day above came with it: the first history left 97 of 99 queue items past due; locally the new one leaves about 50 open, most from the last few days. Found on the way: Cleanpost did not follow a rejected claim the PM sent again, although W-12 says it does; it now does (docs/03). The history carries a version (`DEMO_HISTORY_VERSION`); the preview's release step builds it again, once, when an older version built it, even while an older replay is still running.
- **Amended (Nov 6, 2026):** the Demo clock keeps the world going after the history: on +1, +7 or +14 days the PM's billing batch runs, then the clock moves a day at a time as in the history, each weekday the practices entering that day's visits; then the syncs build the visits, and a `demo.workday` job has the simulated team work the queue as above (a PM with no hold bills the new visits at the next press, after Cleanpost has seen them). A demo given days after the history was built opens, after one press, on a current queue: the new day's reviews, blocks and eligibility issues fresh, most older work done, a few items past due.

## DEC-20 — Reports computed on request; the practice PDF from the browser (Nov 3, 2026)

- **Decision:** RP-01 to RP-05 compute their figures on request from claims, claim events, 835s and work items for the clients in the user's scope (`packages/reports`, tested against direct computations), not from the hourly rollup tables the RP-01 design describes; the rollups come when volume needs them, with the same definitions. ## DEC-21 — Invites: join only with a second factor; links shown where email is off (Nov 4, 2026)

- **Decision:** an invite (S-02) is one row per address with the role and clients, a random 32-byte token sent once in the E-01 link and stored only as its SHA-256, valid 7 days. Resend makes a new token and 7 more days (the old link stops working); withdraw ends it. The unauthenticated A-06 page reads the invite through `app.invitation_by_token` (a security-definer function that takes only a 64-character hash and returns the company, inviter, role, client count and status), so no tenant data is readable without the token.
- **Decision:** joining needs a full sign-in as the invited address, two-factor included. A new person enters their name and password on A-06; that creates their sign-in only, and they go straight to A-03, then back to the invite to join. Someone with a Cleanpost account signs in (A-01 and A-02 carry `?next=`, same-site paths only) and joins. So no membership ever exists without a second factor, which is how A-06's rule "the account is created only after two-factor is set up" is met: the login record exists first because better-auth enrolls TOTP for a signed-in user. Until they join, S-01 shows the invite as pending.
- **Decision:** where email is off (`SMTP_HOST=log`, the preview), S-02 and Resend show the admin the invite link to pass on, so invites can be demonstrated. Only in local and preview (`inviteLinksShown`, packages/config); staging and production send the link by email only, which is how an invite proves the person owns the address. CI runs with email off too, which the e2e uses. A failed send keeps the invite (Resend tries again) and logs only the error code.
- **Also:** an address that is already a member, or already has a live invite, is refused inline; a member of another company can be invited (A-07 lets them switch).

## DEC-22 — What one company's admin can do to a person's sign-in (Nov 4, 2026)

- **Decision:** a person can work in several companies (A-07) with one sign-in, so S-03 keeps each admin's reach inside their own company. Role, clients, deactivate and reactivate change only this company's membership. Sign out everywhere ends only the sessions that have this company open; deactivating does the same at once (and the session check already refuses a deactivated membership). Reset two-factor removes the person's authenticator and passkeys and ends all their sessions, so it is allowed only when they are active in no other company (`app.member_elsewhere`, migration 0016, answers yes or no and only for the caller's own members); otherwise the person uses a recovery code or Cleanpost support.
- **Decision:** the owner's membership and your own are not edited in S-03 (no Edit on those rows; the server refuses too). Deactivating hands their open work items back to the queue unassigned, and removing clients hands back their items for those clients, each through the work-item state machine (`assigned` or `in_progress` -unassign-> `open`, the latter added for this) and audited on the item. Every S-03 change is audited on the membership.
- **Not yet:** primary and backup biller per client (S-03's "Primary"/"Backup" labels and the "choose a new primary biller" prompt) come with client settings (CL-), where the assignment is set; until then the queue assigns as before.

## DEC-23 — Release threshold: a company default, and 100 means review everything (Nov 4, 2026)

- **Decision:** S-04 sets a company default release threshold (`tenants.settings.autoReleaseThreshold`, 90 unless changed). `clients.auto_release_threshold` is now nullable: null follows the company default, a number is the client's own (migration 0017 moved clients at the old default, 90, to null, so nothing changed on the day). The effective threshold is the client-payer override, else the client's number, else the company default, else 90 (`effectiveReleaseThreshold`, used by scrub and release alike).
- **Decision:** 100 means "Review everything", as S-04's design labels it: nothing goes out without a person, even a claim scoring 100. docs/06 said a score at or above the threshold releases, which let a perfect claim through at 100; the more conservative reading of the design wins (`releasesWithoutReview`).
- **Decision:** the simulation ("at N, about X% of the last 30 days' claims would have gone out") uses each claim's first scrub in the last 30 days, from its `scrubbed` event (decision and score); blocked claims count in the total and never release. Lowering the default by more than 5 asks first (G-11). Every change is audited with before and after; changes apply to claims scrubbed after the save. The client table shows the last 30 days' share released without review and the first-pass rate of those claims (RP-01's definitions).
- **Not yet:** the design's "apply transform rules" and "resubmit rejections with an exact fix" toggles are not shown, because neither behaviour exists yet (transforms stay suggestions). Client-payer thresholds stay on client settings (CL-), not built yet.

## DEC-24 — Rules settings: switches, not edits, for built-in rules (Nov 4, 2026)

A company's own rules switch with their `enabled` column and move to a new version. The rules version hash on each scrub includes the switches, so a scrub result says which rules were in play.

The built-in code checks (structural, codes, NCCI, eligibility, duplicates) are listed with their hits and are always on.
- **Decision:** S-06 renders the rule from its JSON (`describeCondition`, `describeOutcome`), so the words cannot drift from what runs. "Test on last 30 days" evaluates the current version of each claim built in the period; it reads claim content, so it is audited (counts only).
- **Not yet:** the structured rule editor and "New rule" (S-06 edit mode), and S-07 proposals from billers' corrections (a nightly grouping job); both next.

## DEC-28 — Notifications: one sweep, in-app first, emails without PHI (Nov 5, 2026)

- **Decision:** alerts come from what is true now, not from events scattered through the code: the worker runs a notification sweep every 15 minutes for each company (`packages/notify`, job `notify.sweep`). It finds a failed connection (its open `sync_failure` item), an 835 out of balance by at least the company's amount ($500 default), a claim within 3 days of its filing limit, a priority 1 item past its SLA, a denial of at least the company's amount ($1,000 default), and a client whose first sync after go-live finished. Each alert is one in-app notification per person (a dedupe key per alert, migration 0022), sent to the audience S-09 names: admins; the assignee (an unassigned item past its SLA tells no one, as it already shows on the queue as overdue); the assignee and admins; or everyone who can see the client. Assignments and write-off decisions keep notifying where they happen.
- **Decision:** E-05 emails go for alerts still true, at most every 4 hours per person, outside quiet hours (timely filing excepted), only where S-09 has email on and the person has not switched urgent emails off in their profile. Admins always get connection-failed and timely-filing emails (S-09 rules). E-04 goes once a day at the digest time, in the company's time zone (7:30 am by default; the user preference can override the time), skipped when the person has nothing open and nothing happened. Its queue is what W-01 shows that person, theirs or nobody's; "yesterday" uses the reports' definitions (RP-07). Emails carry client and payer names, counts and totals, never a patient.
- **Decision:** the timely-filing watch (docs/08) runs in the same sweep: a claim the payer has not accepted, within 14 days of its filing limit, becomes a priority 1 `timely_filing_risk` item (4-hour SLA), audited as `automation:timely_filing_watch`; it resolves itself when the payer accepts the claim or it is voided. W-22, the watch's own screen, comes later.
- **Decision:** E-06 now goes when the first sync after Go live finishes, with its counts, instead of at Go live (DEC-27): the sync job asks for that company's sweep at once, so it arrives within a minute. A client on a PM without an integration is announced at Go live, with no counts.
- **Decision:** the sweep lists companies through `app.active_tenants()`, a security-definer function returning tenant IDs only, the second cross-tenant read the app role has (after `app.sync_candidates()`); everything else runs inside each company's tenant. The worker sends email only when `SMTP_HOST`, `SMTP_PORT`, `EMAIL_FROM` and `APP_URL` are set (the preview's worker uses the web service's values, with `SMTP_HOST=log`); without them, notifications are in-app only.
- **Decision:** S-09 stores one `notification_rules` row per event a company changed (channels, amount) and the digest time and quiet hours in `tenants.settings.notifications`. The time zone is the company's: users have none of their own yet. "Rule proposed from corrections" is listed with its defaults and fires once S-07 proposals exist.

## DEC-29 — Search: what a query is, what a result shows, what the audit log keeps (Nov 6, 2026)

- **Decision:** G-03 reads the query before searching (`packages/domain/src/search`). Under 2 characters it searches nothing. A date (3/7/1984 or 1984-03-07) finds patients born that day. Anything with a digit is a number: patient control numbers (contains), payer claim numbers from 835s (starts with) and trace numbers (contains). Anything else is a name: patients by last name, "Last, First", or first and last in either order (each a prefix), and clients by name. The claims and remittances of the patients found are listed with them. Each group has a limit (patients 6, claims 6, remittances 4, clients 5); a longer query narrows it.
- **Decision:** a patient shows as "Last, F." with an age and their client; the full date of birth only when the query was that date (the G-03 rule). A claim shows its control number, the patient the same way, date of service, payer and status; a remittance its payer, method, trace number, amount and date, and "includes Last, F." when found through a patient. Results keep to the person's clients and role (a viewer finds claims and remittances, not patients).
- **Decision:** every search is audit-logged as `search.performed` with its kind, a hash of the query (the first 16 hex characters of SHA-256 of the lowercased text) and how many it found per group. The text is never kept, numbers included: stricter than G-03's "not the text if it looks like a name", and simpler than deciding what looks like a name. The hash tells repeated searches apart; it sits in the audit log, inside the database like the rest of the PHI.
- **Decision:** recent records (the empty query) come from the person's own `patient.viewed`, `claim.viewed` and `remittance.viewed` audit events, shown with today's labels and only while still in their scope; listing them is audited as `search.recents_viewed`. Nothing about them is kept in the browser.
- **Decision:** commands are a fixed list filtered by role (`apps/web/src/lib/commands.ts`): go to each section the person can open (keeping the client filter), Upload 835 file, Add client, Invite user, Notification settings, the aging report, Profile and security. Upload and invite open their page with the dialog open (`?upload=1`, `?invite=1`). ">" shows commands only; otherwise commands that match by name follow the records, label matches before keyword matches. The two-key shortcuts in the design ("G D") and G-24's "Search" button prefilled from the URL are not built: G-24 sits outside the app shell for now.

## DEC-30 — Coverage and patient corrections in Mode A; W-13 (Nov 6, 2026)

- **Decision:** a claim the scrub blocks only on eligibility (coverage ended, inactive, member not found, other payer reported) gets an `eligibility_issue` item (W-13), priority 1 with a 24-hour SLA (4 hours before a known visit once a PM sends appointments), linked to the patient (`work_items.patient_id`, migration 0023). A claim with any other blocking finding stays `claim_blocked`. Open items made before the change were converted by the migration.
- **Decision:** the PM stays the system of record and the adapter contract has no patient or coverage write yet, so P-02 (coverage) and P-06 (patient details) save the correction in Cleanpost, mark the record as corrected (`source` `user`, or `card` from P-05) and open one task per patient to make the same change in the PM (`updateCoverage`, `updatePatient`; a second correction joins the open task). Each changed field is a `corrections` row (`entity_type`, `entity_id`; member IDs encrypted); the audit event names the fields, not the values. The forms say this before saving.
- **Decision:** the patient's claims are scrubbed again at once, so the biller sees whether the fix works with the payer (P-02 checks eligibility after saving by default; a new payer makes a new claim version). None of them is released while the PM does not show the correction: release refuses with `pm_record_pending` and remembers that the claim waited (`pm_record_wait`).
- **Decision:** the sync leaves a corrected record alone while the PM still shows the old values. When the PM shows the same values, the record is the PM's again, the task closes itself ("PM updated") and the next build releases the claims that waited. If the PM still differs and nobody has the task open (a person closed it), the sync opens a new task saying so: the conflict note, never a silent overwrite (P-06). Adding a coverage that the PM does not have is not built (the sync could not match it).
- **Decision:** W-13 offers Check again (a 270, then the held claims are scrubbed again), Bill the other coverage (when another coverage is confirmed active: a task to switch the visit's insurance in the PM; the next sync rebuilds the claim), Update coverage (P-02), Mark self-pay (the claim is voided "Patient will pay" and a task asks the PM to bill the patient) and Open patient.
- **Decision:** work item summaries and task text never name the patient (the PHI scan over work items enforces it); the queue and panel show who it is from the record, "Last, F." with a link to P-01.

## DEC-31 — Insurance cards: storage, reading and review (Nov 6, 2026)

- **Decision:** card photos (P-04: front required, back optional; JPEG, PNG or WebP by their first bytes, never the file name; up to 15 MB a side) are stored encrypted with the field key before they reach the database (`document_blobs`, migration 0024) in local and preview. Production keeps them in S3 with SSE-KMS behind the same calls (M5). They leave storage only through a five-minute link signed for the person it was made for, which also needs that person's session and client access; every image view is audited (`document.image_viewed`). P-04 Cancel deletes a card nobody confirmed.
- **Decision:** the worker reads each side in a `document.extract` job (document ID only in the payload) with the card reader from `@cleanpost/ai`: Claude on Bedrock where synthetic data or a BAA covers it, routine model, structured output with a confidence per field. In local and preview the stand-in reads only the synthetic cards Cleanpost's simulators make, which carry their own answer in a PNG text chunk; any other photo, a real one included, is unreadable, so the stand-in never guesses. With AI off every card reads as unreadable and people enter the fields by hand (P-02). The 95% field accuracy acceptance (M4) can only be measured with the real reader against the synthetic card set, so it stays open until Bedrock runs on that set.
- **Decision:** what was read is kept on the document with the member ID encrypted like a coverage's; the payer is matched by the client's PM payer names, then payer names and aliases. The front decides; the back fills the fields the front does not show (RxBIN, phone). A front with a deciding field (payer, member ID, group, name) under 0.9, missing, or a payer that matches nothing is a `card_review` item (W-14, priority 3, 48 hours), shown in the queue without PHI.
- **Decision:** a person always confirms (P-05 or W-14; the model reads, deterministic code saves). Fields under 0.9 are marked and the first is focused. Confirm card goes through P-02's path (DEC-30) with source `card`: the coverage changes in Cleanpost, the PM gets its task, claims wait for the PM; eligibility is checked after saving by default. The card becomes `accepted` or `corrected`; what the person changed from the reading is kept as corrections on the document (training data, and the payer aliases to add later: payers are a global table the app cannot write).
- **Decision:** for the demo, local and preview offer "Use a sample card": the mock PM says what the patient's primary card shows (the plan the patient really has, which may not be the one the practice entered, as for a "member not found" claim), and the browser draws a synthetic photo with the app's tokens and its answer embedded. The group number is given 84% confidence so W-14's marking shows.

## DEC-32 — W-22 timely filing: proof, escalation, snooze (Nov 6, 2026)

- **Decision:** W-22 shows the days left in the billing day (Pacific), the deadline, and where the limit comes from: the client's setting for that payer (CL-05) or Cleanpost's 90-day default, said as such. ## DEC-33 — Usage metering (Nov 6, 2026)

- **Decision:** a claim counts once, on the day (Pacific) Cleanpost first released it: released by the adapter, or sent at the PM's batch after Cleanpost approved it (S-11 rule). A correction or resubmission of the same claim does not count again, and a claim the PM sent without Cleanpost's release (`pm_outside_cleanpost`) does not count at all.
- **Decision:** the worker's `usage.meter` job runs nightly (2:41 am Pacific) and once when the worker starts. It rewrites each company's `usage_records` for the last 7 days, so a missed night is caught up, and backfills 120 days for a company with no records yet. Metering a day again gives the same counts.
- **Decision:** S-11 shows usage only until invoicing (M7): claims released per client and mode for this month, last month or the last 90 days, from the records plus today counted live the same way. Plan, invoices and payment method come with the billing provider.
